Every cane in this catalogue that publishes a load figure publishes the same one: 100 kg. That holds for a 120 g stick and for a 750 g model with an armrest. The uniformity matters: the load column will not separate one SKU from another. What separates them is the height range, the standard the model was actually tested against, and the folded length.

## The height rule that decides the SKU

The user stands upright in the shoes they wear outdoors, arms relaxed, shoulders down. The handle belongs at the crease of the wrist. That single measurement is the whole fitting rule for a single-point cane, and it converts straight into the specification you buy: the adjustment range has to cover the 5th to 95th percentile of your market, not the average buyer.

Published spans in this range run from 72 cm to 97.5 cm across ten push-button steps, roughly 2.5 cm between settings. The ten-step column exists for one reason: at 2.5 cm almost every user lands within one step of the correct height. A four-step cane leaves 7 cm between settings and the user compensates by leaning — which is how wrist and shoulder complaints become returns.

Two consequences for a buyer:

- **Height range is a market decision.** A range topping out at 95.5 cm serves the Netherlands or northern Germany only if you accept that taller users sit on the last step. Extending to 97.5 cm costs nothing at the factory and removes the objection.
- **Adult height ceiling.** Above roughly 190 cm, measure the individual before you promise a fit. No cane here is designed past 97.5 cm, and one extended beyond its last locked step is a liability claim rather than a product.

## Why every load figure reads 100 kg

A number that never varies across a product family tells you where it comes from. Here the 100 kg column is the category's working maximum for a single-point cane, declared per model and supported — where it is supported at all — by a test report rather than a calculation.

One model names its standard on the data sheet: the [LM4208TAZ](/en/products/durable-carbon-fiber-walking-stick/), tested to **MZ/T 199-2023**, the Chinese industry standard for single-hand walking aids. That is a real test with a real report, and it is not an EU harmonised standard — so it satisfies a technical file, not a European tender clause asking for EN ISO 11334-4. Know which of the two you are being asked for before you forward the document.

- **Ask for the report, not the number.** A 100 kg declaration naming the model, the sample and the test method is a specification. The same number without a report is marketing.
- **Respect the ceiling.** 100 kg is a maximum, not a working load. For a user near or above it the honest answer is a different device — a quad cane, a rollator, a walking frame — and saying so ends the conversation well instead of starting a claim.
- **Do not forward the GPSR certificate for a cane.** Our certificate TQT1520D368EC is issued against EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012 — furniture seating standards — for a folding shower stool, model LM5108. Canes are not seating, and a supplier who sends that file with a cane order is either confused or hoping you are.

## Folded length, tips and the returns that follow

Returns come from three things, in order: a worn tip, a height setting nobody reset after delivery, and a folding joint that developed play.

**Folded length is a spec.** The four-section models fold to 34–34.5 cm and fit a jacket pocket; the armrest model is a different product for a different user and does not fold small at all.

**The tip is the cheapest component and the most common failure.** The range uses TPR ferrules with a steel insert on the load-bearing models, and the insert is what stops the tip wearing through where the tube meets the floor. Buy tips as a spare-part line — it is the single easiest way to keep a pharmacy or home-care account.

**Handles are a user-segment decision.** Rosewood and sapele sell in gift and pharmacy; PP with an LED handle to travel; the armrest version is for grip weakness, and there the extra 0.4 kg is the point.

## Documents, MOQ and lead time

**ISO 13485:2016, certificate 8623138319** (Huarenlian Certification Service, valid to 2028-06-18). Read the scope line rather than the logo — a QMS certificate for another product family does not cover this order. **An EU MDR 2017/745 Declaration of Conformity, issued per model.** Canes are Class I devices, self-declared: there is no notified body certificate behind them, for us or for any competitor in the class. **FDA establishment registration and device listing**, renewed annually, held per product; registration is not approval.

One IP document is worth knowing: the carbon cane shape is registered as an EUIPO registered Community design, **015134442-0001**. A design registration, not a patent — it matters to a private-label buyer because the shape is defensible in the EU while finish and branding are yours.

MOQ is **200 units** on nineteen of the twenty-two cane SKUs and **300** on the three with the more expensive tube set or the armrest. The published FOB band for the walking aids group is **US$15–25 per unit**, standard colours, export carton, FOB South China. Lead time is **15–30 days** from order confirmation, typically 30% deposit with the balance before shipment. MOQ applies per model and colour, not per order.

## Purchasing recommendation

For a first cane range, take three SKUs: one 34 cm four-section folder at 200 units for travel and gift, one ten-step cane with the 74.5–97.5 cm range for the tall end of the market, one armrest model for the grip-weakness segment every home-care account asks about. That trio clears MOQ on a single PO and covers the three buying reasons behind almost all cane demand.

Then put four lines in the PO: the height range in centimetres, the load figure **with the test report named**, the folded length, and the tip as a spare-part line. ## FAQ

**Is a 100 kg cane enough for every user?** It covers users up to that figure. Above it, move the user to a quad cane or a rollator rather than promising a margin the report does not state.

**Do I need a notified body certificate for a walking cane?** No. Class I devices are self-declared under MDR 2017/745. A tender demanding one for a cane is asking for a document that cannot exist for this device class.

**Can one cane cover a whole family?** No. The height range is the constraint: a 72–95.5 cm cane cannot serve a 190 cm user. Two ranges plus one armrest model is the smallest honest assortment.
