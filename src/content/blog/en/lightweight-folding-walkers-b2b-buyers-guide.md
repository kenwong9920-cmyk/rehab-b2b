Weight in a folding walker is not decided by the frame tube alone. It is decided by three things: what the leg tubes are made of, how the folding joint is built, and how much hardware — wheels, brakes, seat — you bolt onto the platform. Our range of six models sits on a single frame platform with a flat **100-unit MOQ per SKU**, so the buying decision is not "how cheap can I get a walker" but "which three of these six configurations will my market actually reorder".

## Where the weight actually comes from

Three components decide the finished weight, in this order:

**The leg tubes.** On the LM2005T the legs are carbon fibre, the frame is powder-coated stainless steel. Carbon fibre in the legs — not the whole frame — is the pragmatic build: the legs are the longest unsupported span and contribute disproportionately to total mass, while the frame carries the folding stress and is better served by steel. It also solves a paediatric problem that aluminium does not: a printed pattern sits under the clear coat on the carbon tube and does not chip off the way a decal does on a painted aluminium leg.

**The folding joint.** Every model uses the same cross-brace folding mechanism with hardened pivot pins running in nylon bushings, operated one-handed by pulling a release strap. Bushings rather than bare metal-on-metal is what keeps the joint from developing play after a few thousand fold cycles — and joint play is the failure mode that shows up in reviews as "it wobbles now".

**The hardware.** A padded seat, a leather seat, two rubber wheels and a hand brake all add mass in exchange for a function. This is why the same platform produces a 2-wheel rollator and a plain folding walker: the buyer chooses how much function to pay for in weight and in unit cost.

## The specifications that decide returns

Buyers compare frame material and price. End users complain about four other things, so specify them before the order:

- **Brakes vs glides.** The LM2002-S-W1 has hand-operated brakes and rubber wheels for carpet, tile and outdoor surfaces. The seat models without wheels use non-slip rubber tips and are for indoor and paved use. Selling a glider model to a user who walks to the shops is the single most common mismatch we see.
- **Handle height range.** The LM2002-S-W1-FS exists because standard walker handles are too low for tall users, and a stooped posture defeats the point of the device. If your market includes northern European adults, carry one extended-height model rather than telling customers to accept a shorter frame.
- **Seat height and seat material.** A leather seat on the LM2002-S wipes clean and looks like furniture; a padded fabric seat is softer but holds water. For care home use, prioritise wipe-clean.
- **Grip material.** Fabric sleeve grips absorb sweat; foam grips are cheaper and degrade faster. In a humid bathroom or a warm climate the fabric sleeve is the specification that prevents slip complaints.

## MOQ, price and freight economics

Walkers are the simplest category in our catalogue commercially: **a flat 100 units per SKU**, no low-volume variant, because all six models share one frame platform and one set of jigs. The published FOB band is **US$12–20 per unit**, standard colours, export carton, FOB South China, at MOQ quantity. Lead time is **15–30 days** from order confirmation; standard terms are 30% deposit with the balance before shipment.

On freight, the category is bulky rather than heavy, which changes the maths compared with bath chairs. Sea freight from South China to Hamburg or Rotterdam runs roughly 30–38 days; the China–Europe rail route to Duisburg or Warsaw runs roughly 18–25 days and suits mid-volume replenishment better, because you are paying for volume rather than weight. To Japan and Korea, sea transit is 7–12 days and 7–10 days respectively, which makes frequent small replenishments practical — relevant if you are supplying clinics rather than retail. Ask for gross weight and carton dimensions with the quote; both are on the packing list, and neither should be estimated when you build a landed-cost model.

## The compliance file, stated honestly

Every model is CE marked under **EU MDR 2017/745** through our own Declaration of Conformity — Class I devices are self-declared, so there is no notified body certificate behind it, for us or for any competitor selling the same class. The quality system is certified to **ISO 13485:2016 (certificate 8623138319, valid to 2028-06-18)** and **ISO 9001:2015 (8629985173)**, and the facility holds **FDA establishment registration with device listing** — registration and listing, not "approval", which is not a thing the FDA issues for Class I devices.

One distinction worth carrying into your own product documentation: the mechanical strength evidence on our file is **EN 12520:2024 / EN 1728:2012**, tested on the folding shower stool LM5108 under GPSR certificate TQT1520D368EC. Those are furniture seating standards and they cover the seating product, not the walkers. If a tender asks for a walker-specific load test to a medical device standard, that has to be commissioned separately and we will say so before you bid rather than after.

## FAQ

**Why is the MOQ a flat 100 units with no lower option?**
All six models are built on one frame platform with shared jigs, tube cutting and powder-coating batches. A 100-unit run already pays the fixed setup; going below it does not reduce the setup cost, it only spreads it across fewer units.

**Can we mix paediatric and adult models in one order?**
Yes, as long as each SKU meets its own 100-unit minimum. Mixed containers across walkers, canes and bath safety are normal for distributors.

**Is carbon fibre worth it on a walker?**
It is worth it on the leg tubes, which is where we use it. A full carbon frame would raise the unit price well beyond what this category supports at retail, without a proportional benefit in a device the user lifts rather than carries.

**What lead time should we plan for a private-label run?**
15–30 days for production once artwork and colour are approved. Custom frame colour or printed legs add artwork confirmation time, so plan the approval step before your selling season rather than during it.
