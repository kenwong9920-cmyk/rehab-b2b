Almost everything a distributor calls "customization" falls into one of three tiers, and the tier decides the cost, the minimum order and — the part buyers discover late — whether the compliance file still describes the product they are importing. Across this catalogue, all 73 SKUs accept a custom logo, a custom carton and a custom colour. Only 26 accept a custom height range, and 30 accept a change to grip, handle or seat design. Those are different projects, not different checkboxes.

## Three tiers, and where the tooling sits

**Tier one — identity.** Logo printing, carton printing, shipping marks, label and manual text. No new tooling, no new mould, no new test. It is artwork on an existing production run, which is why it is available on every SKU in the catalogue and why it is the only tier that can be added to a 30-unit trial order.

**Tier two — finish and range.** Frame colour, powder-coat finish, anodising, seat colour and material, and a custom height range. Still a new setup, but a repeatable one: colour is a coating batch, a height range is a cut length. Twenty-six of the 73 SKUs support a custom height range, concentrated where it matters commercially — 12 of 21 crutches and 11 of 22 canes, because those two families are sold against percentile body height.

**Tier three — form.** Grip shape, handle shape, seat design, printed seat patterns, wheel type, pad material. This is where a mould or a jig changes, and where the MOQ moves with it. Thirty of 73 SKUs list a design option; walkers are the outlier at 5 of 6, because grip and seat are the parts of a rollator a dealer's customer actually touches.

Two readings of that table are useful and one is a trap.

The useful reading: canes and crutches are the families built for private label. Every one of the 43 SKUs takes logo, carton, colour and — on more than half — a height range and a manual. That is what a distributor's own catalogue needs, and it is available at 200 units per model.

The second useful reading: bath safety has the lowest entry point in the catalogue at 30 units, but the narrowest range of structural options — one SKU with a custom height range, two with a seat or backrest change. If your plan is "our own moulded seat", you are not buying tier two, you are commissioning a mould.

The trap: MOQ is per model and per colour combination, not per order. A four-model order at each model's own minimum is a normal first container. One model below its minimum is not, and no amount of "we will grow" changes the setup cost that sets the floor.

## The compliance question nobody asks

This is the part that separates a private label range from a rebadged one.

A RoHS test report is written against specified models and a series list, and it does not survive an unlisted change. Ours are concrete about this: **RC2503200748** covers the bath chair series — test model LM3200, covering LM5101, LM5103, LM5104, LM3201, LM3202, LM3203, LM3004, LM3205, LM3206 — and **RC2503200997** covers the commode series, test model LM6207, covering LM6201–LM6209 and LM252. Change the seat material on a model inside that list and the substance evidence may no longer describe the article; change the model to one outside the list and it certainly does not.

The GPSR certificate is narrower still. **TQT1520D368EC** is issued under EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012 for a folding shower stool, main test model LM5108, and the certificate states on its face that it applies to the tested sample. It is a real certificate and it is worth having — but it is not a family-wide pass, and a new seat mould is not covered by it.

The Declaration of Conformity behaves differently, and this works in your favour. It is issued **per model**, under MDR 2017/745, as a self-declaration for Class I devices. A private label model that sits on an existing frame, with a different colour, logo and carton, is still named on a DoC — and a new frame is a new DoC. That is the clean line to draw in your own planning documents: colour and carton, no new declaration; new form, new declaration.

Two more things belong in the same conversation. **ISO 13485:2016 certificate 8623138319** (Huarenlian, valid to 2028-06-18) covers the quality system, scope line naming Class I medical devices including walking aids — it does not certify a model, and it does not need reissuing for your brand. And the language obligation sits on the label and the manual, not on the frame: the importer placing the device on an EU market is responsible for information the user can understand. That is why "custom label and manual" appears as a listed option on 12 of 21 crutches and 11 of 22 canes rather than as a favour.

Where the design itself is the asset, protection matters: the carbon fibre cane shape carries **EUIPO registered Community design 015134442-0001**, registered to Guangdong Jinda health care Co., Ltd. A private label built on that silhouette is defensible against a copy; one built on an unprotected shape is not.

## What a private label project costs in time

Lead time on a private label run is **15–30 days from order confirmation**, the same band as standard production, provided the artwork is approved before the run is scheduled. The delays in practice come from three places, none of them the factory: artwork files that arrive as a screenshot instead of vector, a colour specified as "like the competitor's blue" instead of a RAL number, and a manual translated after the cartons were printed.

The published FOB band for the walking aids group is **US$15–25 per unit**, standard colours, export carton. A custom RAL colour or a printed retail carton is a dedicated run and is quoted separately — the honest way to compare two suppliers is at the same specification, which is why the [MOQ and pricing page](/en/moq-pricing/) states the basis rather than a headline figure.

## The brief to send a factory

Six lines, and a project moves a week faster.

1. Model numbers, and whether each is to be taken as-is or modified.
2. Which tier: identity only, finish and range, or a form change.
3. Colour as a RAL or Pantone reference, never as a sample photo.
4. Carton: dimensions you need, print colours, and whether the carton is retail or transit.
5. Manual and label languages, with the market named — German for Germany is not German for Austria plus Switzerland when the label carries an importer address.
6. Whether the artwork is yours or needs to be created, and who holds the design rights.

## Recommendation

Start at tier one on a model already in production, at its own MOQ, and put your logo, carton and manual on it. That validates your market, your artwork workflow and your importer paperwork for the price of a standard order.

Move to tier two only on the SKUs that sell. Colour is the cheapest differentiator in this catalogue — available on all 73 SKUs — and a custom height range is the one that actually wins a tender, because it is the specification buyers cannot get from a stock catalogue.

Reserve tier three for one product, not a range. A new grip or seat design is a mould, a test cycle and a compliance conversation, and it should be spent on the model that will carry your brand's name. The rest of the range can stay on existing platforms indefinitely — the [OEM and private label page](/en/oem-odm/) sets out how the two are quoted, and the [bath safety category](/en/products/category/bath-safety/) is the family with the lowest entry point if you want to test the process on a low-cost item first.

## FAQ

**Can I put my logo on a 30-unit order?** On the bath safety family, yes — that is the lowest MOQ in the catalogue, and logo and carton are tier-one changes with no tooling. Canes start at 200 and walkers are flat at 100, so a 30-unit trial is a bath-safety trial, not a cane trial.

**Does a custom colour require a new test report?** Not automatically, but it is the wrong question. The right one is whether the model you are shipping is named in the series list of the report you are relying on, and whether the change touches a tested material. Colour on a coated frame usually does not; a different seat material usually does.

**Can you supply the manual in German and French on the same carton?** Yes, and it is a listed option rather than a special request. Name the markets and the importer details at quotation, because the label carries an address — a German-only label is not usable for an Austrian importer without a reprint.
