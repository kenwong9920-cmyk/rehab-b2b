Folding is not a feature, it is a trade: you give up weight and gain 34 cm of stowage. In this catalogue the fixed carbon cane weighs 120 g and the folding ones start at 330 g — 2.75 times the mass for a stick that fits in a 35 cm carton. Seventeen of twenty-two cane SKUs are folding. That ratio is not a coincidence: it is what the retail channel in Europe and Japan asks for, and it is why the fixed cane has become the specialist item rather than the default.

## The 200 grams you pay for a hinge

Weight is published on twelve of the twenty-two cane SKUs, and the split is clean. The three non-folding models that publish a weight are 120 g (LM4914TA), 155 g (LM4904TA) and 165 g (LM4907). The carbon folding models land at 330 g (LM4903TAZ), 360 g (LM4907TAZ, LM4904TAZ) and 420 g (LM4907TB). The armrest variant, LM4907TCF, is 750 g — the armrest, not the hinge, is what costs there.

The delta is 200–300 g, and that delta is not cosmetic. A cane is carried in the hand for the length of a walk, so gram counts are the one specification an end user can feel without reading a label. It is also the reason a fixed cane still sells at the top of the range: the user who walks two kilometres a day and never folds the stick should not carry a hinge.

What the hinge buys is the middle of the load path. The LM4907TAZ-001 is built in four sections, which means three joints between handle and tip, and each joint is a clearance that has to stay tight. That is a maintenance characteristic, not a defect: folding canes need the push-button locked and the joints checked, and they are the models where a loose segment is felt as a wobble before it is seen.

## 34 cm folded, and the carton that follows

Five SKUs publish a folded length: 34 cm on the LM4903TAZ, 34.5 cm on the LM4907TAZ and LM4904TAZ, and "about 14 inches" (35.6 cm) on two further models. The two that also publish a package size give 35 × 16 × 7.5 cm — 4,200 cm³ per unit.

That number is where a cane order changes shape, because freight is bought on volume as often as on weight. At a volumetric divisor of 6,000, a 4,200 cm³ carton bills as 0.70 kg; at 5,000, the divisor most express carriers use, it bills as 0.84 kg. The cane inside weighs 330–360 g. On air freight or express you are paying for the carton, not the cane — more than twice the actual mass. On sea freight the same arithmetic applies in cubic metres: 1,000 units is 4.2 m³ before any outer carton.

Two consequences, both procurement decisions:

- **A folding cane is a volume product.** If your channel is e-commerce with parcel delivery, the 35 cm carton is the cost driver and the 200 g weight penalty is irrelevant to your landed cost. Negotiate the carton, not the gram.
- **A fixed cane is a stocking decision.** There are no joints to check at goods-in and nothing to lock before use, so a clinic or a rental fleet that inspects every unit spends less labour per cane. That, not the 200 g, is why fixed models survive in the rehab channel.

## Tube diameter and the part that wears out

The LM4907TAZ publishes 22.2 mm upper tube and 19.1 mm lower tube — 7/8 inch and 3/4 inch, the imperial sizes the whole accessory trade is built around. That matters after the sale, not before it: a 19.1 mm lower tube takes a standard 19 mm ferrule, so the replacement tip is a commodity part a distributor can stock by the thousand rather than a factory order.

Tips in this range are described as reinforced TPR with a metal insert (LM4907TB), an all-terrain pivot tip (LM4208TAZ) and oversized non-slip rubber (YH00069). The metal insert is the detail worth specifying: a plain rubber ferrule wears through at the rim first, and a worn rim is how a cane slips on a wet tile. Tip replacement is the largest after-sales part category in walking aids and the cheapest way to keep a user on the same cane for years.

Height adjustment is push-button and tool-free on the folding models, ten steps on the ones that publish the count. Published spans run 72–95.5 cm, 72–96.5 cm, 72.5–95.5 cm and up to 74.5–97.5 cm on the armrest model. Two models publish only "ten levels" without a span, which is the same gap described in the [cane height and load specification](/en/blog/walking-cane-height-load-rating-b2b-guide/): the level count is a manufacturing fact, the span is the specification.

## What the documents actually cover

Six of the twenty-two cane SKUs publish a 100 kg load figure. The strength standard named in the cane records is **MZ/T 199-2023**, and it appears on the LM4208TAZ. What is not on file matters too: the GPSR certificate **TQT1520D368EC** covers a folding shower stool, main test model LM5108, tested to EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012 — it says nothing about canes, and no supplier should let a buyer assume otherwise.

What a cane order does come with is the per-model **Declaration of Conformity under MDR 2017/745** as a Class I self-declaration, and **ISO 13485:2016 certificate 8623138319**, whose scope line names Class I medical devices including walking aids. The carbon fibre cane shape also carries **EUIPO registered Community design 015134442-0001** — relevant if you intend to build a private label on that silhouette rather than a generic one.

If your market asks for a specific cane standard — an EN or ISO reference in a tender document — ask which one and confirm coverage before the order, at quotation stage.

## Recommendation

Buy the fixed cane if the user walks far and never folds; buy the folding cane for every channel where the product is shipped to a home. The 200 g penalty is real but it is not what your customer feels — what they feel is whether the stick goes into a bag.

For a first order, take one folding model at 200 units and one fixed model at 200, both in the standard colour, and put them side by side in your own catalogue. The pair tests the two halves of the market for the price of one SKU, and it tells you within a season which of your customers is buying portability and which is buying weight. Then extend the winner to a second finish rather than a second model — colour is the cheapest differentiator in this range, and the joint, the tip and the tube diameter are already common across the folding family.

## FAQ

**Is a folding cane as strong as a fixed one?** The published load figure is the same, 100 kg, on both the folding and the fixed carbon models. The difference is in maintenance: a folding cane has joints that must stay locked, and the user has to check them. Strength at the factory is not the same claim as strength after two years of folding.

**Can I ship folding canes by air without paying for air?** No — a 4,200 cm³ carton at a 5,000 divisor bills 0.84 kg against a 0.36 kg cane. If air freight is your main lane, the carton is the negotiation, not the product weight.

**Do the canes take standard replacement tips?** The published lower tube diameter is 19.1 mm, which takes a standard 19 mm ferrule. Confirm the diameter on the model you order, because a non-standard tube turns a commodity part into a factory order.
