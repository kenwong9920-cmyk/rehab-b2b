A shower chair almost never fails at its rated load. It fails at the leg joint, at the cut end of a tube, or at the screw that holds a seat bracket — three places where the finish stops and bare metal starts. The material question is really a wall-thickness and finish question, and that is why a 1.2 mm aluminium frame rated at 150 kg outlives a heavier steel frame rated at nothing in particular.

## Which is better, aluminium or stainless steel?

Neither, on material alone. Aluminium wins on weight and cost per unit, which is why it carries most of the volume in this category. Stainless steel wins at two extremes: the very heavy user, where load dominates everything else, and the institutional buyer who pressure-washes chairs daily. The decision is made by load rating, by who lifts the chair, and by the cleaning regime.

## What actually corrodes

Shower water is not plain water. It carries chloride from soap and softened mains supply, it sits in crevices, and it dries slowly on a chair that stays in the cubicle. Three failure points follow:

- **Cut tube ends.** A frame tube is cut, drilled and then coated. Any coating that runs before drilling leaves a bare edge inside a leg, and that is where rust starts — invisible, until it stains the tray.
- **Joints and welds.** Every weld is where chlorides concentrate, and a painted weld is protected only until the paint chips at the foot.
- **Fasteners.** Stainless screws in an aluminium frame are fine; zinc-plated screws in any frame in a wet room are not. This is the cheapest thing to specify and the most commonly skipped.

## 1.2 mm versus 1.4 mm

Wall thickness decides what a frame can do, and it is the number catalogues usually omit.

| Wall | Typical load | Where it belongs |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2 mm (AL6063) | 150 kg | Home use, standard adult, folding travel chairs |
| 1.4 mm (aluminium alloy) | 500 lb / 227 kg | Care-home and clinic use, bariatric entry level |
| Reinforced stainless | 350 kg | Bariatric and institutional, where weight is not the constraint |

The 1.2 mm frames are not weak: 150 kg covers the great majority of users. But a 1.2 mm leg dents before it bends, and a dented leg no longer sits flat on the tray — that is the "one leg wobbles" return.

## Weight in the hand: 1.3 kg against 3.2 kg

Weight is not a spec-sheet nicety; it decides who can use the chair unaided. The lightest frame here is 1.3 kg with a 51×40 cm seat, light enough to lift out of a cubicle one-handed while standing. A 3.2 kg frame with a backrest and armrests needs two hands and is best left in place.

For a care home that is irrelevant — nobody moves the chair. For a pharmacy channel selling to the user it decides whether the product gets used at all. Ask which channel the SKU serves before choosing the frame.

## Seat height and seat size: the fit numbers

**Seat height** runs 74–86 cm across the adjustable range, in five to seven settings depending on the model. The correct height puts the user's feet flat on the tray with the knees at roughly a right angle — measured, not guessed. It also has to suit the transfer: a wheelchair user transfers down onto a seat level with the chair, while a user standing from a walker wants a higher seat.

**Seat footprint** matters more than seat width alone. The 51×40 cm seat on the [LM5101KD-S](/en/products/stainless-steel-shower-chair-3/) and the 42.5×32.5 cm PU seat on the [LM5106KD](/en/products/500lbs-capacity-anti-slip-shower-chair/) are not interchangeable products, and the 38×40 cm seat on the LM5103D is where a distributor gets complaints from larger users. Publish the seat dimension in centimetres with the load rating next to it — together they are what the end user needs.

## What the documents actually cover

Three files do the work here, and two of them are routinely overstated.

**EU RoHS test report RC2503200748**, issued 2025-04-01, covering the bath chair series under Directive 2011/65/EU and (EU) 2015/863. This is a substance-compliance report. It is not a strength test and it says nothing about load.

**EU GPSR certificate TQT1520D368EC**, issued 2026-06-10 against **EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012** — the European seating strength standards — plus GPSR 2023/988. The main test model is a folding shower stool, LM5108, and the certificate states plainly that it applies to the tested sample and does not assess the whole production. That limitation is normal; a supplier presenting an EN 12520 report as proof that every frame in the catalogue has been tested is misrepresenting it.

**ISO 13485:2016 certificate 8623138319** (Huarenlian Certification Service, valid to 2028-06-18) plus an EU MDR 2017/745 Declaration of Conformity per model. Shower chairs and bath stools are Class I devices, self-declared — no notified body is involved for anyone in this class, so a tender asking for a notified body certificate is asking for a document that cannot exist.

Three documents, three different claims. Keep them apart and you will never be the buyer who forwarded a RoHS report as a load test.

MOQ across bath safety runs from **30 to 500 units per model** — the widest range in the catalogue, and the reason is moulds: a standard moulded seat starts at 30 units, a new seat mould or a custom frame colour moves the entry point up to 300–500. Lead time is **15–30 days** from order confirmation, typically 30% deposit with the balance before shipment.

MOQ applies per model and colour, not per order: two frames at their individual minimum is a valid first container.

## Purchasing recommendation

Take three frames, not seven: one 1.2 mm folding aluminium chair at 300 units as the volume SKU, one 1.4 mm aluminium chair at 500 lb as the step-up for care homes, and one stainless heavy-duty at 350 kg for the bariatric enquiry that arrives once a quarter and is always urgent. That trio covers the load bands without carrying four slow-moving middle SKUs.

Then put five lines in the PO: frame material **and wall thickness**, load rating with its test basis, seat dimensions in centimetres, the chair's own weight, and stainless fasteners. ## FAQ

**Are aluminium shower chairs rust-proof?** Aluminium does not rust, but it corrodes in chloride-rich water, and the coating at a cut tube end fails first. Specify anodised or powder-coated tube with sealed ends.

**Is stainless steel always the better buy?** Only above roughly 150 kg or in institutional cleaning regimes. Below that it adds weight and cost the user cannot feel.

**Do I need an EN 12520 test for a shower chair?** It is a legitimate strength reference and it supports a GPSR file, but it is a furniture seating standard, not an MDR requirement. Ask which model was tested, and read the sample limitation before citing it.
