A walking aid entering the US is a Class I medical device, and almost all of them are exempt from 510(k) premarket notification. That sentence is where most buyers stop reading, and where most compliance problems begin — because exemption from premarket notification is not exemption from regulation. The establishment must register, every device must be listed, the label must comply, and the registration lapses every year unless somebody renews it.

## Registered, listed, cleared: three words, three different things

The single most common error in supplier paperwork is treating these as interchangeable. They are not, and a supplier who uses them loosely is telling you something about how their own file is kept.

| Status | What it means | Who obtains it | Applies to walking aids? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establishment registration | The facility is registered with FDA and has an FDA registration number | The manufacturing establishment, renewed annually | Yes — required |
| Device listing | Each device/commercial distribution combination is listed against that registration | The establishment, per device | Yes — required |
| 510(k) clearance | FDA has reviewed a premarket notification and found substantial equivalence | The submitter, per device | No, for most walking aids — they are exempt |
| "FDA approval" | A premarket approval (PMA) for high-risk Class III devices | The submitter | No — the term does not apply to walking aids at all |

That last row matters commercially. Writing "FDA approved" on packaging for a Class I walking aid is not marketing licence, it is an inaccurate claim that a professional buyer, an Amazon compliance reviewer or a customs broker will spot immediately. The correct phrasing is **FDA registered and listed**. Our own registration and listings are supplied as document numbers with the file package, because a number on a website cannot be checked and a number in a document can.

## What the factory registers, what you file

Registration is a manufacturer obligation. Market access is an importer obligation. Buyers who conflate the two discover the gap at the port.

| Item | Owner | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Establishment registration | Manufacturer | Annual; the number your broker asks for first |
| Device listing | Manufacturer | Registration without listing is a common gap in competitor paperwork |
| US agent for a foreign establishment | Manufacturer | A US-based agent must be named; this is not the importer |
| Declaration of conformity / classification statement | Manufacturer | States the classification and the regulation the device meets |
| Label artwork to 21 CFR 801 | Both | Manufacturer drafts; your name and address appear as importer of record |
| UDI and GUDID entry | Importer, as labeler | Class I devices still need UDI labelling and a GUDID record |
| Entry filing (CBP Form 7501) | Importer | Your broker files; we supply invoice, packing list and HS code |
| Bond, duties and merchandise processing fee | Importer | Cash flow item, not a unit-cost item |

Where this bites is the UDI row. Class I devices carry UDI obligations, and the GUDID record is normally created by whoever is the labeler. If your label carries your brand, you are the labeler, and no amount of factory documentation creates that record for you.

## The renewal window that quietly breaks listings

FDA establishment registration must be renewed every year, in the window **1 October to 31 December**. A registration that is not renewed in that window expires, and the device listings attached to it stop being active until it is fixed.

The practical consequence for importers is a calendar problem, not a paperwork problem. Orders placed in November for December production arrive in January, and if the supplier's registration was allowed to lapse over the new year, the container sits while somebody in another time zone finds a login. Two habits prevent it: ask for a screenshot of the current registration status before booking a Q4 order, and put the renewal in your supplier scorecard alongside the certificate expiry dates.

The same logic applies to our ISO 13485 certificate, **number 8623138319**, issued 19 June 2025 and valid to 18 June 2028. Certificate expiry is knowable years in advance; registration renewal is annual and therefore forgettable. Both belong on the same tracking sheet.

## Labels, intended use, and the claim that changes your classification

A walking aid's classification follows its intended use, and the intended use is whatever the label and the listing say it is. This is not a technicality — it is the mechanism by which a compliant product becomes non-compliant without a single design change.

Three claims reliably move a product out of the exempt space or into a different regulatory conversation: therapeutic outcomes ("corrects gait", "treats balance disorders"), sterility claims, and claims that turn an aid into a measured medical device. A rollator described as a mobility aid is a Class I exempt device. The same rollator described as a rehabilitation device that restores walking function is a claim your regulatory consultant should read before the artwork goes to print.

Our own product documentation stays deliberately mechanical on this point: load ratings, tube materials, adjustment ranges, wheel type, fold dimensions. The [aluminium underarm crutch LM4206S2](/en/products/adjustable-aluminum-crutch/) is described by what it is — multi-size adjustment covering 155–195 cm, replaceable underarm pads, non-slip tips — not by what it will cure. That restraint is what keeps a catalogue exportable to fifty markets without redrafting claims per country.

## Dual-market compliance: MDR and FDA on one product

Distributors who sell in both Europe and North America usually carry one product file with two regulatory front ends. The two regimes are not parallel, and the differences decide which document you ask a supplier for.

| Question | EU (MDR 2017/745) | US (FDA) |
|---|---|---|
| Class of a walking aid | Class I | Class I |
| Pre-market review | None — self-declaration | None — 510(k)-exempt |
| Core document | Declaration of Conformity naming the models | Establishment registration plus device listing |
| Who is legally responsible for the claim | The manufacturer named on the DoC | The establishment and the labeler |
| Unique device identification | UDI under MDR, entered in EUDAMED | UDI in GUDID, created by the labeler |
| Renewal rhythm | Continuous; DoC updated when the device changes | Annual, 1 October – 31 December |
| Local presence | EU importer with name and address on the label (GPSR 2023/988) | US agent for the foreign establishment |

Read the table as a buying instruction rather than a compliance lecture. When a supplier sends you a CE declaration and calls it "FDA equivalent", the correct response is to ask for the registration number and the listing separately — they are two lookups in two public databases, and both take under five minutes.

## Procurement recommendation

For a first US-bound order of walking aids, ask for four things before the proforma: the establishment registration number, the device listing for each model on the order, the label artwork showing your company as importer of record, and the classification statement. Then verify the registration in FDA's public database yourself — it is free and it takes one query.

On the product side, keep the first order narrow. Our 21 crutch and cane SKUs run a **100–500 unit** minimum with an FOB band of US$15–25, so three SKUs at their own floors or two at 200 units each is a sensible first consignment, on 15–30 days production and 30% deposit. ## FAQ

**Does a walking aid need 510(k) clearance?**
Generally no. Most canes, crutches, walkers and rollators are Class I devices exempt from premarket notification. The exemption assumes the intended use stays inside the exempt description — add a therapeutic claim and the analysis has to be redone.

**Is an FDA registration number the same as approval?**
No. FDA does not approve Class I devices. Registration identifies the establishment; listing identifies the device; neither is a finding of safety or effectiveness. Writing "FDA approved" is a claim error, not a shortcut.

**Can the factory handle the UDI for us?**
The factory can print a UDI carrier if you supply the DI. The GUDID record is created by the labeler, which is normally the brand owner or importer of record, so this stays with you.

**What happens if the registration lapses?**
Listings attached to an expired registration stop being active, and entries can be held. Renewal happens between 1 October and 31 December each year, which is exactly when most importers are busy with peak-season freight — hence the value of checking before you book.
