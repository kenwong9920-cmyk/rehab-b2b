The expensive part of importing mobility aids is not the unit price. It is the paperwork you did not know you had to own, and the fixed cost of a shipment you split into three. A walker or a rollator is a Class I medical device in both the EU and the US, so the process starts before the purchase order and does not end when the container is unloaded. Here is the sequence, with the owner of every step marked.

## The nine steps, and who owns each one

Nothing in this table is optional, and the sequencing matters more than the speed of any single step. Buying a sample and booking freight in the same week is how importers end up with a container of a model that failed inspection.

| # | Step | Owner | Output | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Factory verification | Buyer | ISO 13485 certificate checked against the issuing body's register | 3–5 days |
| 2 | Sample order | Both | 1–3 units by air, paid at sample price | 7–10 days |
| 3 | Specification lock | Both | Signed spec sheet naming model, tube wall, load rating, carton | Before the PO |
| 4 | PO and deposit | Buyer | 30% deposit against a proforma naming every model | 1–2 days |
| 5 | Production | Factory | Finished goods, pre-shipment inspection booked | 15–30 days |
| 6 | Inspection | Buyer | AQL report, carton weights and dimensions verified | 1–2 days |
| 7 | Booking and documents | Both | Invoice, packing list, B/L, declaration of conformity, test reports | 3–5 days |
| 8 | Customs entry | Buyer | Duty and import VAT paid, entry filed by your broker | 1–3 days |
| 9 | Post-market duties | Buyer | Registration as importer, labelling checks, complaint file | Ongoing |

Step 1 is where most first-time importers save two weeks and lose a year. Our quality system certificate is **ISO 13485:2016, number 8623138319**, issued 19 June 2025 by Huarenlian Certification Service and valid to 18 June 2028. Verify the number and the scope wording on the issuer's register, and check that the legal entity on the certificate matches the business licence on the invoice. A certificate that names a trading company while the invoice comes from a factory is a mismatch worth asking about before any money moves.

## The document map: what the factory issues, what you file

Buyers routinely assume the factory handles compliance. It handles manufacturing compliance; the market-facing obligations belong to whoever places the device on the market. Splitting the list in two is the single most useful exercise in the whole import process.

| Document | Issued by | Why you need it |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 13485 certificate | Factory | Supplier qualification file; required by most EU and Gulf tenders |
| Declaration of Conformity (EU MDR 2017/745) | Factory, as legal manufacturer | Must name the exact models you buy — not "the range" |
| Test reports (GPSR, RoHS) | Factory via test house | Our GPSR report is **TQT1520D368EC**, tested to EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012; RoHS report **RC2503200748** to 2011/65/EU and (EU) 2015/863 |
| CE marking and label artwork | Factory supplies, buyer approves | Importer name and address must appear on the device or its packaging |
| EUDAMED registration as importer | Buyer | MDR requires importers to register as economic operators |
| Import declaration and duty | Buyer's customs agent | Classification, origin, VAT |
| Packaging EPR registration | Buyer | Register before the first delivery, not after |
| UDI / GUDID entry (US) | Buyer as labeler | Class I devices still need UDI labelling |

The GPSR point is the one that catches German and Dutch importers most often. **Regulation (EU) 2023/988** requires a responsible economic operator established in the EU, with a name and a postal address on the product or its packaging and a traceability file kept for ten years. If your label shows only the Chinese manufacturer, the product is not compliant no matter how good the test report is.

## What landed cost actually contains

Landed cost is not FOB plus freight. Five layers sit between the FOB figure on the proforma and the number your accountant books, and the middle three are the ones that move.

| Layer | Basis | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| FOB unit price | Per unit, at MOQ | Our walkers run US$12–20, crutches US$15–25, ex-works South China |
| Inland and origin charges | Per consignment | Consolidation, export declaration, documentation |
| Ocean or rail freight | Per CBM or per container | Mobility products are bulky, so volume — not weight — sets the cost |
| Duty | CIF value, by HS code | Crutches and canes usually sit under heading 6602; walking frames are more often classified as orthopaedic appliances under 9021. Your agent makes the binding call, but ask for the code we put on the invoice |
| Import VAT | CIF plus duty | Recoverable if you are VAT-registered, but it is cash out for 4–10 weeks |

Sea freight from South China to Hamburg or Rotterdam runs roughly 30–38 days; the China–Europe rail route to Duisburg or Warsaw runs 18–25 days and suits mid-volume replenishment of bulky cartons. Air is for samples only — a rollator's carton is mostly air, which is exactly what air freight charges for.

## Four errors that turn a first container into a loss

**1. Treating the MOQ as a single number.** It is per model and per colour. Our six walker models all quote **100 units** flat because they share one frame platform; the 21 crutch and cane SKUs run **100–500** because carbon-fibre tube sets and tip tooling are not shared across the whole range. Three models at their own minimum is a valid order; one model below its minimum is not.

**2. Splitting a small order across shipments.** Export documentation, pre-shipment inspection, loading supervision and forwarder fees are per consignment. Ninety units across three LCL bookings pays those four times over. One mixed consignment at 300–500 units is almost always cheaper landed than three small ones, even at a worse unit price.

**3. Accepting a generic declaration of conformity.** A mixed container of five SKUs needs a declaration that names all five models with their classifications. A declaration for "our walking aids" is worth nothing at a customs audit or a tender clarification, and it is the document buyers most often forget to check until it is too late.

**4. Budgeting on FOB and discovering packaging EPR afterwards.** In Germany the packaging register (LUCID) must be registered before the first delivery, and the carton weight on your packing list becomes the reporting basis. For bulky mobility products, the licence fee is not a rounding error — it belongs in the landed-cost sheet from the first quote.

## A first-order structure that survives customs

For a distributor opening a walking-aid line, the structure that holds up commercially is three SKUs at their own floors in one consignment: the [two-wheel rollator LM2002-S-W1](/en/products/lightweight-black-2-wheel-rollator/) with hand brakes and a quick-fold frame as the volume line, the [tall-adult folding walker LM2002-S-W1-FS](/en/products/lightweight-foldable-walker-tall-adults/) as the differentiated model that generalist competitors do not carry, and one crutch SKU such as the [LM4206S2 aluminium underarm crutch](/en/products/adjustable-aluminum-crutch/) covering 155–195 cm to round out the pallet. Terms are 15–30 days production, 30% deposit, balance against a copy of the bill of lading.

Two things to ask for that most importers only think of later: the carton dimensions and gross weight per SKU, so you can compute container utilisation before you book; and the declaration of conformity covering the exact model list on the order. Both are free at quotation stage and expensive to fix later.

## Procurement recommendation

Buy the first container on documentation quality, not on the lowest FOB number. A supplier who can hand you a verifiable ISO 13485 number, a model-specific declaration of conformity and a test report naming the standard is a supplier whose next three orders will also be clean. Start with one mixed consignment, keep two SKUs at their minimum rather than five below it, and put the packaging EPR and VAT cash flow into the landed-cost sheet before you sign.

## FAQ

**Do I need my own importer registration in the EU?**
Yes. Under MDR 2017/745 you register as an economic operator in EUDAMED, and under GPSR 2023/988 you are the responsible person whose name goes on the label. Neither can be delegated to the Chinese manufacturer.

**Is a walking frame a medical device or furniture?**
It depends on the intended-use statement you put on the label. Sold for rehabilitation or mobility support, it is a Class I medical device and needs CE marking under MDR. Sold as a household aid without a medical claim, the analysis changes — which is why the artwork is a compliance document, not a design detail.

**How much can I fit in one container?**
Mobility products fill on volume long before weight. Above roughly 500 units the 40HQ is the efficient unit, and a mixed container across categories is normal once every SKU clears its own minimum. Ask for carton dimensions per model before booking, because a rollator carton and a crutch carton use space very differently.

**What is the realistic timeline for a first order?**
Six to nine weeks door to door: one week of verification and sampling decisions, 15–30 days production, one to two weeks of inspection and booking, then 18–38 days in transit depending on rail or sea.
