The minimum order quantity is not a negotiating position. It is a setup-cost calculation that the factory has already done for you, and the 30-to-500 spread inside a single catalogue is a map of how much dedicated tooling each model needs. Our commode chair range starts at 30 units on a folding steel model and reaches 500 on a padded one, and the difference between those two numbers is not sales policy — it is welding jigs, a seat mould and a sewn leather subassembly.

## Where the floor actually comes from

Four cost blocks set the minimum, and only one of them scales with quantity:

1. **Tube cutting and bending.** A program is written once per tube profile. Thirty pieces and three hundred pieces pay the same programming time.
2. **Welding jigs.** A commode frame is a fixture. A new frame geometry means a new jig, and a jig is amortised across the run that uses it.
3. **Powder-coating batch.** Standard colours are coated in batches serving several orders. A custom RAL colour becomes a dedicated batch, and the minimum rises with it.
4. **Carton printing.** A stock carton with your shipping mark costs a fraction of a full-colour printed box, which needs its own print run.

That is why the three commode models sit where they do. The folding **LM6108F-2298** holds 150 kg on a high-carbon steel frame with five height levels and folds tool-free — it starts at **30 units**, because it is a stock colour, a stock carton and an existing jig. The stainless steel **LM6209KL** with the patented flip-adjust leather backrest and double-layer HD-PE flip-up seat starts at **300 units**: stainless welding is a different fixture, the PE seat is a mould, and the leather backrest is a sewn subassembly bought in with its own supplier minimum. The padded **LM6108F-157** sits at **500**.

## What a 30-unit order really costs you

The unit price stays inside the published band at MOQ — we do not run a public discount ladder, so haggling on the per-unit figure is not where the money is. The real penalty of a small order is the **fixed cost per shipment**: export documentation, pre-shipment inspection, loading supervision and forwarder charges are per consignment, not per unit. Split 90 units across three small LCL shipments and you pay those fixed costs three times.

The efficient structure for a small first order is therefore one mixed consignment, with each SKU at its own minimum. Mixed containers are normal for distributors: combine categories freely once every SKU clears its own floor. Above roughly 500 units the 40HQ becomes the efficient unit for mobility products, because they are bulky rather than heavy — the container fills on volume long before it reaches a weight limit.

## Three order structures that work

| Situation | Structure | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| Testing a market | 3 SKUs at individual minimum (e.g. 30 + 100 + 50), one LCL | Highest cost per unit, lowest capital at risk |
| Distributor ramp-up | One 40HQ, 3–4 SKUs at 500+ | Best cost per unit, needs sell-through confidence |
| Tender or care-home supply | Spec-led model choice, buffer stock held | MOQ is irrelevant next to meeting the technical annex |

The mistake we see most often is a first order built around unit price instead of consignment structure. Moving from three small orders a year to one mixed container usually cuts landed cost more than any negotiation on the unit figure, because you are removing fixed cost rather than shaving margin.

## What the number does not cover

MOQ is a production figure, and production is only part of the landed cost. Lead time is **15–30 days** from order confirmation, with 30% deposit and the balance before shipment against a copy of the bill of lading. Sea freight to Hamburg or Rotterdam runs roughly 30–38 days; the China–Europe rail route to Duisburg or Warsaw roughly 18–25 days, which suits mid-volume replenishment of bulky cartons.

Two obligations sit with you as the importer and are easy to overlook when budgeting. **EU packaging extended producer responsibility** requires the importer to register and report the packaging it places on the market — in Germany that means registration in the LUCID register before the first delivery, and the carton weight on your packing list becomes a reporting input. And the **Declaration of Conformity** has to name the exact models on the order: a mixed container of five SKUs needs a declaration covering all five, not a generic one.

## Procurement recommendation

For a first commode chair order in the DACH region, the structure that survives contact with a real market is: **30 units of the folding LM6108F-2298** as the entry SKU, **100–300 units of one height-adjustable model** as the volume line, and **one 300-unit run of the LM6209KL** if your customers are care homes that specify stainless steel and a flip-up seat. Three SKUs, three different floors, one consignment.

Then treat the MOQ as a signal rather than an obstacle. A model quoting 30 units is telling you it uses existing tooling — which also means it is the model least likely to be discontinued, and the safest thing to put on a first container. ## FAQ

**Is MOQ per order or per model?**
Per model and per colour combination. Three SKUs each at their own minimum is a valid order; one SKU below its minimum is not. The order total is irrelevant to the floor.

**Why is a stainless steel commode chair 300 units when a steel one is 30?**
Different welding fixture, a moulded PE seat and a sewn leather backrest — three separate setup costs, each with its own supplier minimum. The steel folding model reuses tooling that already exists.

**Can we start below 30 units?**
No, and we would rather say so than quote a price we cannot hold. If your volume is below the floor, tell us the target and we will name the models that can meet it instead of discounting the ones that cannot.

**Do you offer tiered pricing above MOQ?**
The published band is the range we quote from at MOQ quantity. Above that, price moves with material and specification rather than with a published ladder — send the volume and we will quote it directly.
