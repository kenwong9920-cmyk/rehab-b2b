The rated load on a shower chair is a static figure for the frame and seat assembly. It is not evidence that the chair has been tested to a medical device standard, and treating the two as the same thing is the most common documentation mistake we see from new importers. Our bath-safety range runs from 150 kg (330 lb) to 350 kg (770 lb), with one 500 lb aluminium model in between — and the differences that decide returns are seat width, height range and how the backrest and armrests attach, not the headline number.

## What the figure covers — and what it does not

The 350 kg rating on the two LM5109 models comes from a reinforced white stainless steel frame with a 6-level flat-tube height adjustment and angled non-slip feet with an EVA thermal mat under the seat. On the 500 lb LM5106KD the load path runs through a 1.4 mm aluminium wall, a PU seat with drainage holes and an anti-slip texture, a backrest adjustable in two levels (38.5 × 19.5 × 2.5 cm), and foam-wrapped armrests on a figure-8 tube that adjust in width at two levels per side. Those features hold a heavier user steady while sitting down — the moment the chair is loaded hardest.

What we will not do is imply that any of these ratings is a medical device test result. Here is the documentation that exists for this category, stated plainly:

- **GPSR Certificate of Compliance TQT1520D368EC** — issued by TQT, covering the folding shower stool LM5108, tested to **EN 12520:2024** and **EN 1728:2012**. Those are furniture seating standards. They are the correct basis for CE marking under the EU General Product Safety Regulation, and they are not medical device standards.
- **RoHS report RC2503200748** — covers the bath chair series LM5101, LM5103 and LM5104 (test model LM3200). Restricted substances only; it says nothing about strength.
- **EU MDR Declaration of Conformity** — our own declaration under 2017/745 for Class I devices, naming the models it covers. No notified body is involved at Class I, for us or for any competitor.
- **ISO 13485:2016 certificate 8623138319** — the quality system behind the production line, valid to 2028-06-18, scope covering walking aids, bath safety seating, commode chairs and toilet safety rails.

If your tender or your distributor specifies a mechanical strength test to a medical device standard such as ISO 7176 or a national variant, that test has to be commissioned separately. We would rather quote the lead time for it than hand you an EN 12520 report and let you assume it covers the requirement. The full document set, with the numbers you can check against the issuing body's register, is on the [certifications page](/en/certifications/).

## Matching a rating to the user

Three purchasing rules we apply when quoting this category:

1. **Buy margin, not a match.** A rated load equal to the user's weight is not a specification, it is a coin toss. We recommend at least 20–30% headroom for daily home use, which is why a 100 kg user is usually better served by a 150 kg chair than by a 120 kg one.
2. **For care homes, 150 kg covers the standard resident; keep one 350 kg model.** Institutional tenders increasingly require at least one bariatric unit per bathroom cluster, and retrofitting one later costs more than including it in the first order.
3. **Where the load is applied matters more than the number.** A user who pushes down on an armrest to stand puts a lateral load on the frame that a static seat rating does not describe. That is the case for the extra-wide models with the reinforced cross bar, not for the lightest chair in the range.

Height range is the specification buyers forget: 6 or 7 levels is what lets one chair work for a 150 cm and a 190 cm user in the same facility — and why the 500 lb LM5106KD has seven settings where the 150 kg models have five.

## FAQ

**Is a higher load rating always a better chair?**
No. Above roughly 250 kg the frame gets heavier and the base wider, which makes the chair harder to move and to store in a small bathroom. Match the rating to the user population, not to the highest number in the catalogue.

**Do you supply load test reports for these models?**
The strength evidence on file is EN 12520:2024 / EN 1728:2012 for the folding shower stool LM5108, plus a batch load and visual inspection report with every shipment. A test to a medical device standard is a separate commission — tell us the standard and we will quote the lead time.

**Can the 350 kg models be private-labelled?**
Yes. Frame colour, seat and backrest material, logo printing and packaging are all customisable on the LM5109 platform at 100 units per SKU. A custom RAL colour becomes a dedicated coating batch, so the minimum rises with it.

**Which model fits a wet room with a floor drain and no shower tray?**
Any model in the table — all of them use angled non-slip feet rather than suction cups. For a wet room we point buyers to the LM5103D or LM5101D, because the aluminium frame tolerates permanent humidity better than a painted steel one.
