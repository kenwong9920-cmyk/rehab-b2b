Two measurements decide which crutch you can sell, and neither of them is the tube material. The handgrip has to sit at the wrist crease of a standing user, and an axillary crutch has to leave two to three finger widths of clearance below the armpit. Get those wrong and you have a crutch that is returned for a sore wrist or, worse, for nerve pressure under the arm. Everything else — carbon versus aluminium, folding versus fixed, 100 kg versus 200 lb — is a decision made after those two numbers are fixed.

## The three numbers that fix a crutch

**Handgrip height.** The user stands upright, arms hanging relaxed, shoulders down. The handgrip then belongs at the height of the wrist crease. This is the single rule that spans axillary and forearm crutches, adult and youth. Because it is measured, not guessed, it converts directly into the adjustment range you should be asking for: a crutch whose grip range does not cover your market's 5th to 95th percentile is not a SKU, it is a sample.

**Underarm clearance.** On an axillary crutch the pad goes two to three finger widths — roughly 4–5 cm — below the axillary fold, never into the armpit. Weight belongs on the palms, not on the axilla. Crutch-induced radial nerve pressure has a name (crutch palsy) and one cause: a crutch that is too long, so the shoulder shrugs up to meet the pad. As a first approximation an axillary crutch is body height minus 41 cm, then adjusted by the two rules above — treat the arithmetic as a starting point and the handgrip rule as the final word.

**Load rating, with its basis stated.** A number without a basis is not a specification. "200 lb" is not the same statement as "100 kg per pair", and a supplier who will not say which one applies to the model in front of you is telling you something about the rest of the file.

## Tips, folding and the returns that follow

Returns on crutches come from three things, in order: worn tips, a height setting nobody re-set after delivery, and a folding joint that develops play.

Tips are the cheapest component and the most common failure. The range uses thickened TPR with a steel or iron insert on the elbow models and reinforced ferrules on the underarm models — the insert is what stops the tip wearing through where the tube meets the floor. Ask for tips as a spare-part line; a cheap tip sold as an accessory stops a whole crutch going in the bin.

Folding is a spec, not a feature. Folded lengths run from 44.5 cm on the carbon elbow crutch to 61.5 cm on the LM4202B, and 45 cm on the three-section underarm models. A crutch that folds to 45 cm fits a day bag; one that folds to 61.5 cm does not, and the buyer who assumed otherwise is your return.

## Documents: what the file must contain before the container ships

Three documents do the work, and one of them is routinely faked, so it is worth naming precisely what we hold.

**ISO 13485:2016, certificate 8623138319** (Huarenlian Certification Service, valid to 2028-06-18). The scope line names walking aids — check the scope wording, not the logo, because a QMS certificate for another product family does not cover this order.

**An EU MDR 2017/745 Declaration of Conformity, issued per model.** Crutches are Class I devices, self-declared: there is no notified body certificate behind them, for us or for any competitor selling the same class. The DoC must name the model you are buying and the standards applied; ours is supplied with the document package rather than published.

**FDA establishment registration and device listing**, renewed annually, held per product. Registration is not approval — Class I devices are not "FDA approved" by anyone. If a supplier uses that phrase for a crutch, audit the rest of their paperwork.

One trap that catches experienced buyers: a GPSR certificate is not a crutch document. Our GPSR certificate TQT1520D368EC is issued against EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012 — furniture seating standards — for a folding shower stool, model LM5108. Those standards do not apply to crutches, and a supplier forwarding that certificate for a crutch order is either confused or hoping you are.

## Order quantity, price band and lead time

MOQ runs from **100 to 500 units per model** depending on frame type: the folding all-age model starts at 100, the multi-size aluminium axillary models sit at 200, and the double-crutch and three-section folding models at 300–500. The published FOB band for the walking aids group is **US$15–25 per unit**, standard colours, export carton, FOB South China. Lead time is **15–30 days** from order confirmation, typically on 30% deposit with the balance before shipment.

The MOQ is per model and colour combination, not per order. Three models at their individual minimum is a valid order and is how most first containers are built; one model below its minimum is not.

## Purchasing recommendation

If you are building a first crutch range for a pharmacy or home-care channel, take two SKUs, not six: one multi-size aluminium axillary model covering 155–195 cm, and one folding carbon elbow crutch for the younger, mobile patient. That pair covers the two clinical situations behind almost all crutch demand and clears MOQ without tying up capital in four slow-moving variants.

Then put four lines in the PO: the height range in centimetres your market needs, the load rating and its basis per model, the folded length, and the tip as a spare-part line. ## FAQ

**Is 200 lb enough for an axillary crutch?** It suits a user up to 91 kg. Above that, ask for the model-level rating in writing and for a batch load test, because most catalogue pages in this category quote a household figure rather than a tested one.

**Can one model cover 155 to 195 cm?** Yes — the LM4206S2 does exactly that with multi-size adjustment. It cannot serve a 145 cm user, which is why a youth model in the 95–155 cm band belongs in the range as well.

**Do I need a notified body certificate for crutches?** No. Class I devices are self-declared under MDR 2017/745. A tender demanding a notified body certificate for a crutch is asking for a document that cannot exist for this device class.
