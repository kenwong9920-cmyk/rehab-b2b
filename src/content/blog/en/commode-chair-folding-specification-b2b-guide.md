"Heavy duty" is the most over-used phrase in this category and the least useful. A commode chair is bought against four figures — seat height range, folded width, bucket dimensions and rated load — and every one of them is checkable before the sample arrives. What follows is how those four figures behave across our seven commode models, where the range stops, and which lines to put in the purchase order so the container that lands matches the catalogue you printed.

## The four figures that decide the order

**Seat height, in steps.** Five height levels is the standard across this range, and it is not decoration. A commode that is 3 cm too high is unusable for a user who cannot push up from a chair; the same chair 3 cm lower is fine. Because the adjustment is stepped rather than continuous, ask for the actual level spacing, not just "adjustable".

**Folded width.** This is the number that decides whether the product sells at all in home care. The LM6108F series folds to **17 cm** across the frame — narrow enough to stand behind a bathroom door or in a wardrobe. Buyers who compare on weight instead of folded width routinely pick the heavier chair and then discover it will not fit anywhere in the flat it was bought for.

**Bucket dimensions.** The LM6301KG uses an oval PP bucket at **33 × 28 × 13 cm** with dual handles, and it is removable. A bucket that is too shallow is a cleaning problem; one with a single handle is a handling problem for a carer wearing gloves. Both are specified, not negotiated after the fact.

**Rated load.** The whole commode range is rated at **150 kg / 300 lb**. That figure is uniform across steel and stainless models, and it is the ceiling — see the section below on what happens above it.

## One frame, three roles

Most of this range is bought as a commode and used as something else. The LM6108F-2298 combines bedside commode, over-toilet frame and shower chair in one steel frame with a removable basin; the LM502-S does the same with no assembly at all. For a distributor, that is the argument that survives a price comparison: three saleable applications from one SKU, one carton, one spare-parts list.

What makes the second role possible is the backrest. The patented flip-back leather backrest on the LM6108F-2727 and LM6301KG folds out of the way so the chair can be pushed over an existing toilet; the flip-adjust version on the stainless LM6209KL gives a reclining angle instead. Neither is a gimmick — a fixed backrest blocks the over-toilet use, which is exactly the use that makes a second purchase unnecessary.

## Where 150 kg is the ceiling, and what to do above it

No commode chair in this catalogue is rated above 150 kg. If a tender specifies 200 kg or 350 lb, the honest answer is that the requirement is met by a different product family, not by a beefed-up commode. Our heavy-duty capacity sits in the bath safety line, where the [extra-wide bariatric shower chair](/en/products/white-extra-wide-heavy-duty-shower-chair/) is rated **350 kg / 770 lb** on a reinforced stainless frame — but it is a shower chair without a bucket, and selling it as a commode would be a specification error that surfaces at the first audit.

Say that in the quotation rather than after the order. A distributor who discovers the ceiling from a customer complaint has already printed the catalogue.

## Steel choice: high-carbon, powder-coated, or stainless

All three finishes are rated 150 kg, so the decision is about the room, not the load.

High-carbon steel with a powder coat is the volume choice: stiff, cheap to form, adequate in a dry bedroom. White powder coat is standard for care-home interiors because it shows soiling immediately, which is the point.

Stainless — the LM6209KL — is for wet rooms, communal bathrooms and any market where the chair is cleaned with chlorine-based products several times a day. Powder coat fails at the welds and the folded hinge first, and a chip that starts at a weld becomes a rust line. In hospitals and nursing homes the stainless model is not an upsell, it is the lower total cost.

## Documents before the container

The quality certificate that matters is **ISO 13485:2016, certificate 8623138319** (Huarenlian Certification Service, valid to 2028-06-18). Its scope explicitly names commode chairs — that is the sentence to read, because a QMS certificate scoped to another product family does not cover this order.

On substances, **RoHS report RC2503200997** covers the commode chair series **LM6201–LM6209 and LM252**, test model LM6207. Note what that means for the LM6108F models: they sit outside the series named on that report, so ask for the report that names your model rather than accepting one from a sister series. A RoHS report is not a strength claim either — it is a hazardous-substance result, not a load test.

In the EU, commode chairs are Class I medical devices under MDR 2017/745 and are self-declared: the Declaration of Conformity is issued per model, reference supplied in the document package. They are also consumer-facing products under GPSR 2023/988, which is why our GPSR certificate TQT1520D368EC exists — tested to EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012. Read its coverage line before forwarding it: it is issued for a folding shower stool, model LM5108, and does not extend to commode chairs. Seating standards transfer by product, not by category.

## Order quantity, price and lead time

MOQ is **30 to 500 units per model**, price band **US$10–15 per unit** FOB South China for standard colours in export cartons, lead time **15–30 days** from confirmation. As everywhere in this catalogue the minimum applies per model and colour, not per order, and mixed containers are fine once each line clears its own minimum.

Freight on commode chairs is decided by carton cube, not weight: a chair packed at 17 cm folded width cubs out well, the same chair shipped assembled does not. Ask for carton dimensions with the quotation and build landed cost on those.

## Purchasing recommendation

For home care or pharmacy, open with the folding LM6108F-2727 at its 30-unit minimum and put the LM6301KG behind it — one entry price point, one better-margin model with the flip-back backrest and the oval bucket. For nursing homes and hospitals, invert it: the stainless LM6209KL plus the LM6207L for heavier residents, with the steel models as the overflow line.

Either way, specify four things in writing: seat height levels in centimetres, folded width, bucket dimensions and handle count, and rated load with the standard it was tested to. ## FAQ

**Can a commode chair be used as a shower chair?** Only if it is specified for it. The LM6108F-2298 and LM502-S are built as three-in-one units with water-resistant seats; a bedroom commode with a leather seat and fixed backrest is not, and using it in a wet room destroys the frame fastest.

**Why is the MOQ 30 on one model and 500 on another?** Tooling. The 30-unit models share jigs with frames already running in volume, so no new setup is charged. The 500-unit model carries its own setup, and its minimum pays for it.

**Is a RoHS report a safety certificate?** No. RoHS covers restricted substances under 2011/65/EU and (EU) 2015/863. It says nothing about whether the chair holds 150 kg — that is a batch load test, a separate document.
