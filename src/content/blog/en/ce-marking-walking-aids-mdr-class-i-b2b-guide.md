Three documents arrive in most Chinese walking-aid quotations, and all three get called "CE". Only one makes a crutch or a cane lawful to sell as a medical device in the EU: the manufacturer's own Declaration of Conformity under MDR 2017/745. The other two — a GPSR certificate and a RoHS test report — are genuine documents with real numbers, and they are routinely presented as if they were the first. They are not interchangeable, and telling them apart takes about twenty minutes.

## What Class I actually obliges the manufacturer to hold

Walking aids are non-invasive devices, so under MDR Annex VIII they land in **Class I**. That classification is the whole answer to a question buyers keep asking: there is no Notified Body in this chain. Class I devices that are non-sterile and have no measuring function are self-certified. The manufacturer issues the Declaration of Conformity on its own authority, under its own quality system, and no third party signs it.

That is also the fastest fraud check you have: a supplier offering a Notified Body certificate for a walking stick is either selling a different class or does not understand the one they sell.

What the manufacturer must hold, and what each piece does:

| Document | Issued by | What it proves | Public register |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU Declaration of Conformity (MDR Annex IV) | The manufacturer, signed | Named models meet the General Safety and Performance Requirements | No — the easiest document to fake |
| Technical documentation (MDR Annex II & III) | The manufacturer | Risk management to EN ISO 14971, verification, validation, GSPR checklist | No — released to an authority |
| ISO 13485 **8623138319** | Huarenlian, Chinese body | How the factory is run. Names no model | Yes |
| GPSR certificate **TQT1520D368EC** | TQT | General product safety, tested to **EN 12520:2024** and **EN 1728:2012** | Report TQT1520D368E traceable |
| RoHS **RC2503200748** / **RC2503200997** | GD-SCT | Restricted substances only. Nothing on strength | Report on request |
| EUIPO design **015134442-0001** | EUIPO | The look of the carbon fibre cane. Not compliance | Yes |

The GPSR certificate is the one that causes the most damage, because it is real and it is impressive-looking. It covers a folding shower stool, model LM5108, tested to two European furniture seating standards. It permits CE marking to demonstrate compliance with the General Product Safety Regulation — a different regulation from the MDR, for a different purpose. Send it to a German distributor as your medical device evidence and the conversation ends there.

## The standards gap buyers discover at the tender stage

Most supplier pages leave this out. Our strength evidence on file is EN 12520:2024 and EN 1728:2012 — **furniture seating standards**, correct for the product they were commissioned for, and not the standards that describe a crutch.

The standards a European tender or a large distributor may actually name for this category are different ones: **EN ISO 11334-1** for elbow crutches, **EN ISO 11334-4** for walking sticks, and **ISO 11199-1** or **-2** for walking frames and rollators. If your technical annex cites one of those, the existing EN 12520 report does not answer it. That is a separate test commission, and we quote the lead time rather than hand over an adjacent report and let you assume it covers the requirement.

Knowing which test you are asking for is worth money. A distributor who finds the gap after the container lands pays twice — once for the commission, once for stock it cannot legally sell as a medical device.

## What the document has to say about your models, specifically

A Declaration of Conformity is not a house document. It names the devices it covers, and a model not named on it is not covered. For the crutch range that means one declaration per platform: the carbon fibre elbow crutch **LM4401** (1.2 mm wall, twelve levels, 91–118 cm) sits on a different line from the aluminium underarm **LM4206S2** (155–195 cm, multi-size) or the wood-grain axillary **LM4208** at 2.54 lb (1.15 kg).

Three things to check in the document itself:

- The **legal entity** must match the ISO 13485 certificate and the invoice. Not a trading company — the entity that actually manufactures.
- The **class** must read Class I and the **regulation** 2017/745, not 93/42/EEC. A directive reference on a 2026 document means the template was never updated.
- The **standards applied** must be listed by number. "Complies with all applicable European standards" is not a list.

## What you cannot delegate to us

MDR moved obligations onto the importer, and no supplier can absorb them for you:

| Obligation | Owner |
|---|---|
| DoC naming the models | Manufacturer |
| Technical file for the authority | Manufacturer |
| Manufacturer SRN in EUDAMED | Manufacturer |
| **Importer SRN in EUDAMED** | **Importer** |
| **Importer name and address on the label** | **Importer** — we print your block |
| UDI-DI on label and packaging | Manufacturer |
| Instructions in the member-state language | Both — we translate, you approve |
| Vigilance and incident reporting | Both — routes through you |

Two practical consequences. Your SRN should exist before the container ships, not after — retrofitting an importer record to a device already on the market is a mess. And the label language is not a translation exercise: for Germany, Austria and Switzerland it is German, checked by the distributor before release.

## A twenty-minute verification routine

1. **Ask for the PDF, not the logo.** A certificate image with the number cropped out is unverified, and the cropping is usually deliberate.
2. **Check the number against the issuer.** ISO 13485 8623138319 should resolve with a scope that names walking aids. If the scope says "hardware" and nothing else, it does not cover your order.
3. **Read the standard numbers, not the badge.** EN 12520 is furniture. EN ISO 11334-1 is a crutch. The document tells you which test was actually run.
4. **Match the legal entity** across certificate, business licence and invoice.
5. **Get the version date in writing.** DoCs are revised; a stale version naming a discontinued model is a common find.

## FAQ

**Do Class I walking aids need a Notified Body?**
No. Non-sterile Class I devices with no measuring function are self-certified under MDR 2017/745 — the manufacturer signs the DoC and no third party is involved. A Notified Body certificate offered for a crutch means the class is being misstated.

**Is a GPSR certificate enough for CE marking?**
Enough for CE marking under the General Product Safety Regulation, which is a different regime from the MDR. For a product sold as a medical device, the MDR Declaration of Conformity is the document that matters. Holding both is normal; presenting only the GPSR one as device evidence is the error.

**We are the importer. What do we file?**
Your EUDAMED actor registration and SRN, your name and address on the label, and the language-specific instructions for the member states you sell into. We supply the manufacturer SRN, the DoC, the technical documentation index and the artwork with your importer block set.

**Can you test to EN ISO 11334-1 if our tender requires it?**
Yes, as a separate commission. We quote the lead time and cost against your model list before you commit, rather than imply the existing reports cover it. Send the exact standard and edition from the tender annex.
